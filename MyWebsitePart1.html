Diwali

Diwali is the Hindu festival of lights, with variations celebrated in other Indian religions such as Jainism and Sikhism. It is associated with the spiritual victory or triumph of Dharma over Adharma, light over darkness, good over evil, and knowledge over ignorance. Diwali is celebrated during the Hindu lunisolar months of Ashvin (according to the amanta tradition) and Kārtika between around mid-October and mid-November. The celebrations generally last five or six days.

Diwali: 'Most eaten Food' on this auspicious day

Diwali food is an essential part of the Festival of Lights, bringing families together over a spread of traditional snacks and sweets. Popular savory treats include samosas, crisp pastries filled with spiced potatoes and peas , murukku, a crunchy South Indian snack made from rice and urad dal flour , and namak para, flaky diamond-shaped bites enjoyed during festivals . Sweet delights like kaju katli, a melt in the mouth cashew sweet symbolizing prosperity , and besan ladoo, rich balls made from gram flour and ghee , add festive sweetness to the table. Many of these dishes—such as murukku, mathri, chivda, and shankarpali—are prepared ahead of time and shared with loved ones, making Diwali a celebration filled with flavor, tradition, and togetherness

Popular Diwali traditional clothes include sarees, lehengas, salwar-kameez, Anarkali suits for women, and sherwanis or kurta-pajamas for men, often crafted in silk, brocade, zari, or embroidered fabrics to reflect the festival's cultural richness. These garments are worn to symbolize purity, prosperity, and new beginnings during the Festival of Lights.
